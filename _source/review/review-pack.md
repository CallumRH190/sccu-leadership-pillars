# Review pack: SCCU Leadership Pillars hand-over to James

## Situation
Callum is a leadership consultant. His client is SCCU Group, a UK apprenticeship training provider. James Pease is the COO. Scott Riddell is the CEO. Callum has coached both for two years. James has five heads of department reporting to him. The stated problem: James is managing too much and putting out too many fires because the heads are not stepping up.

Callum has had a tool built, "Leadership Pillars". Once a month each head self-scores six pillars from 1 to 10, with one dated example each. James scores the same head without seeing their scores. Both sets are revealed together, biggest gaps first. They agree a score per pillar in the room, pick one focus pillar and set one real assignment. A one-page summary comes out. 7 is the standard on every pillar; nothing marks 7 while a head is self-scoring.

The six pillars and their opposites: Resilience (Reactivity), Decisiveness (Inaction), Ingenuity (Rigidity), Assertiveness (Avoidance), Communication (Assumption), Enthusiasm (Indifference). Each has three traits (never scored, used as questions) and a "counterfeit" (looks like it but isn't).

The current tool is a prototype with five fictional sample heads. Data saves only in the viewer's browser. There are no logins yet. Scores are developmental and not linked to pay.

## Why video: what is said (transcribed from the final audio; the recogniser makes small errors)
Before you learn how to use it, here's why it's built in the way it is. James, this is built to give you three things: fewer fires landed on your desk. heads who deal with problems at their own level. and one fair standard you can hold every head to. You already have the scorecards, they tell you what happened. They don't tell you why. head can hit the numbers and still leave problems for you to clean up. Pillars cover the part the scorecard can't see. How your heads behave. There are six pillars, resilience, decisiveness, ingenuity, assertiveness, communication and enthusiasm. They came from your own words and our conversations. They were checked against the leadership research that I've done and tested on five real situations at SCCU. Each one runs from its opposite. So a head isn't good or bad at communication, for example. They're somewhere between assumption and communication. This month. And each has a counterfeit, something that looks like the pillar Isn't Cheerleading without delivery looks like enthusiasm. Now the order. The head scores themselves first, but why? Because the moment they see your number, they're going to stop thinking and start agreeing. Scoring first makes them look honestly at their own mump. Then you score them without seeing theirs for the same reason. If you saw their number, it would pull yours towards it. Two independent views are worth far more than one view and an echo. Then you reveal both together. Where you agree, move on. Where there's a gap, that's your conversation to be had. they're scored higher than you. they have a blind spot. If you scored higher than them, they're underrating themselves and you get to tell them. You never average the two. You agree the score in the room. Every school needs one dated example that turns opinion into an evidence. And the score is this month, not a label on the person. A hard month drops it, and a good month brings it back. Seven is the standard for a head here. people are pass marked and they score themselves just above it. So nothing marks seven while they're scoring. They read the behaviour and pick what's true. The line only appears once both scores are in. Two more rules. One case counts on one pillar. So a single bad day can't sink three scores. and the heads keep trusting the numbers. And every session ends with one focus. One real Assange. Done in the job. Behaviour changes by doing. and it grows. Later the same six pillars become the questions you can ask when you hire. Heads can run it with their own teams and Scott sees the whole leadership picture on one screen. 20 minutes a month. Perfect. That's the why. Next, how to use it.

## How video: narration lines in order
- This is Leadership Pillars. Here's how to run one session, start to finish.
- Click your name, top right. This menu switches who you are.
- Choose Elena Marsh, to become a head.
- Press Start scoring.
- Drag the marker, and watch the description change underneath. Stop where it sounds like your month.
- Then type one real example, and add its date. No example, no score.
- Do that for all six pillars, check them on the review page, and press Submit. The scores are now locked.
- Now switch back to yourself.
- Open Session, and pick Elena. Her scores are in, but hidden from you.
- Score each pillar yourself, with your own example and date. We'll skip ahead through the other five.
- When all six are done, press Lock my scores.
- Now, with Elena beside you, press Reveal.
- Both sets appear together, with the widest gap at the top. Green is Elena. Pink is you.
- Click that pillar. Her example and yours sit side by side,
- with the questions to ask underneath.
- Talk it through, then click the score you both agree. Never just the average.
- Repeat for all six, then move on.
- Pick one focus pillar. The tool suggests one.
- Choose an assignment, or write your own, and set a due date.
- Press Finish, and you both get a one page summary.
- To start again, click your name, and choose Reset the demo.
- That's the whole session. Switch person, self-score, score blind, reveal, agree, and focus. Try one yourself before Tuesday.
